## Stress on karma-yoga?
### Sequence
> kRShNa stressed on karma yoga

No - he also spoke with great emphasis on two+ other yogas.  
bhagavad-gItA is not a subhAShita book, from which one can cherry-pick verses in isolation as per one's taste. Several commentators having presented it in this way have confused many simpletons. 

karma, jnAna and bhakti yoga-s are presented in that sequence - each in great detail, just as a mathematics textbook may have parts on arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry and calculus. 

Just because someone has no capacity or taste for anything beyond arithmetic, it does not mean that there is nothing higher than arithmetic; or that what may be achieved with calculus may be achieved just with arithmetic.

### UpaniShads and brahma-sUtra-s
UpaniShads and brahma-sUtra-s famously emphasize jnAna and then brahma-vidyA-s (aka upAsana-s, corresponding to bhakti-yoga); and not karma-yoga. bhagavad-gItA, being a summary of those, ultimately does the same.

## Emphasis on karma
Of course, the following shANkara contention (held by all traditional maTha-s) is groundless -  

> One should have abandoned all karma via sarva-karma-sannyAsa (eligible for only v1 males) in this life or a previous one, in order to attain mokSha.  

So is similar prattling by people influenced by them (eg. gauDIya-s).  
karmas (especially those prescribed for one's varNa and Ashrama) should never be abandoned. To that extant, yes, there is emphasis on karma. But that is not karma-yoga.

## Anti-intellectualism?
### Not kRShNa
> kRShNa was of course a philosopher but not a philosopher who was interested in debating clubs.

This is correct given that kRShNa is the omniscient God, who instructs those who deserve instruction, as per their capacity and taste.

However, what applies to kRShNa does not apply to others, who may need to counter the misrepresentations of his words as part of their visheSha-dharma.
